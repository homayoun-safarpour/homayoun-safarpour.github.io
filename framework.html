Framework

Evidence-first agent assurance.

Eight controls for auditing what an AI agent did. Each one comes from findings in standards, regulation and research: 83 findings from 91 sources, each with a quote that was checked on the source's own page. For each control: the question an auditor asks, where my tools stand today, and the clause it relates to.

Related to, not compliant with: these controls give evidence for duties; they do not certify anything. The summaries are mine; follow each link for the source's own words.

1

Scope and permissions

Partial9 verified findings

May the agent do this, with these arguments, after what it has already done?

Our check
Gate 2: every call replayed through Microsoft's policy engine, default deny. Tool names only; not arguments or call order yet.
Related to
OWASP Agentic Top 10: ASI02 tool misuse. EU AI Act Art. 9. NIST AI RMF MANAGE.
What the research says
  • Audit MCP authentication and authorization carefully because chained tool calls operate with delegated user permissions and can amplify a single vulnerability. OWASP Foundation, 2026 ↗
  • AgentCap checks authorization independently of the model and validates limited capability tokens before tools execute actions. arXiv, 2026 ↗
  • AgentGuard supports runtime auditing through a policy interface backed by checks for both individual tool calls and risks spanning multiple tools. arXiv, 2026 ↗
2

Behaviour evidence

Covered8 verified findings

What did the agent actually do, step by step, not only at the end?

Our check
Gate 1: the trace against a reviewed rubric and a pinned baseline; a forbidden call stops the run. Covered for tools and their order.
Related to
EU AI Act Art. 15. NIST AI RMF MEASURE 2.5.
What the research says
  • Agents must expose what they accessed, what they did and why, while allowing their behaviour to be controlled at runtime. OWASP Foundation, 2026 ↗
  • Audit individual tool-use steps, not just final outcomes, because mistakes can cause irreversible side effects. arXiv, 2026 ↗
  • Evaluate execution time as well as accuracy, because planning, reflection, and judging calls account for most agent latency. arXiv, 2025 ↗
3

Records and traceability

Covered8 verified findings

Can someone else find the record of the run and check it?

Our check
Gate 3 and the sha256 fingerprint of every file each gate read. Covered.
Related to
EU AI Act Art. 12 record-keeping.
What the research says
  • The proposed project would link agent actions to agent identities so auditors can trace automated activity and its outcomes. National Institute of Standards and Technology, National Cybersecurity Center of Excellence, 2026 ↗
  • The draft recommends documenting why a deployment decision keeps misuse risk within the organization's risk tolerance. U.S. AI Safety Institute, National Institute of Standards and Technology, 2025 ↗
  • Developers must maintain an audit trail covering the operation and lifecycle management of the system's models, datasets, and prompts. European Telecommunications Standards Institute, 2025 ↗
4

Human oversight and hand-over

Partial9 verified findings

Did a person get a usable record, and could they really step in?

Our check
Gate 4: the hand-over record must name what was proved, what is pending, when to stop. Checks the record, not whether it is true or whether anyone intervened.
Related to
EU AI Act Art. 14 human oversight.
What the research says
  • Institutions should consider accountable human oversight of high-impact decisions, supported by auditable documentation showing how AI outputs influenced final outcomes. Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, 2026 ↗
  • The authors argue that effective human oversight may require evidence-based measures against overreliance on AI, not merely awareness of that risk. Cambridge University Press, European Journal of Risk Regulation, 2025 ↗
  • Do not treat a human being in the loop as proof of effective oversight because automation bias can prevent people from recognising or correcting AI errors. Georgetown University, Center for Security and Emerging Technology, 2024 ↗
5

Evaluation validity and judges

Partial28 verified findings

Is the score itself trustworthy: checked against people, repeated, free of order and wording bias?

Our check
Gate 5: agreement between judges and each judge's consistency. No human-labelled anchor or bias probes yet. The research is loudest here.
Related to
NIST AI RMF MEASURE 2.13.
What the research says
  • Check the statistical assumptions behind evaluation scores because some methods produce invalid uncertainty estimates. National Institute of Standards and Technology, 2026 ↗
  • Auditors should validate an LLM judge against human judgments for the particular task before relying on its scores. Association for Computational Linguistics, 2025 ↗
  • Auditors should test safety judges for sensitivity to superficial wording because apologetic language alone can substantially skew their verdicts. Association for Computational Linguistics, 2025 ↗
6

Adversarial testing

Partial7 verified findings

Does a determined attack succeed, including instructions hidden in what the agent reads?

Our check
Gate 6: a red-team report read and counted. Model scans only; not attacks through the agent's tools.
Related to
OWASP Agentic Top 10: ASI01 goal hijack. EU AI Act Art. 15. NIST AI RMF MEASURE 2.7.
What the research says
  • Auditors can use the guide's attack scenarios and mitigations to structure adversarial testing against documented LLM application risks. OWASP Foundation, 2026 ↗
  • AISI found universal jailbreaks for every system it tested, so auditors should not treat installed safeguards as proof against misuse. UK AI Security Institute, 2025 ↗
  • Test whether malicious instructions in external tool results can redirect an agent into unauthorized actions. arXiv, 2024 ↗
7

Monitoring after deployment

Gap4 verified findings

Does the agent behave in real use the way it did in tests?

Our check
None yet. The clearest gap: models can tell when they are being tested.
Related to
EU AI Act Art. 72 post-market monitoring. NIST AI RMF MANAGE 4.
What the research says
  • Audits need evidence from monitoring after deployment because controlled tests cannot capture changing real-world conditions. National Institute of Standards and Technology, 2026 ↗
  • The concept note identifies monitoring for environmental changes beyond verified operating conditions as a trustworthiness feature for critical infrastructure AI. National Institute of Standards and Technology, 2026 ↗
  • When using the monitor as audit evidence, distinguish individual incidents from news coverage because multiple articles can describe the same event. Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development, 2023 ↗

Accountability and governance

Partial10 verified findings

Who answers for the result, and against which rule?

Our check
The report itself: each tool, version, verdict and exit code; the EU requirements map. Evidence for a decision, not the decision.
Related to
EU AI Act Art. 17 quality management; harmonised standard EN 18286:2026. ISO/IEC 42001. NIST AI RMF GOVERN.
What the research says
  • An audit should cover risks across interacting agents as well as individual agents, matching the guide's coverage of both environments. OWASP Foundation, 2026 ↗
  • For auditors, the key finding is that existing cybersecurity practices need adapting to address agent-specific security risks. National Institute of Standards and Technology, 2026 ↗
  • The guide uses threat modeling to connect agent-specific threats with mitigations, providing a basis for auditing whether relevant risks have been addressed. OWASP Foundation, 2025 ↗
Words used here
Trace
The recorded steps of one agent run: what it was asked, which tools it called, what it replied.
Gate
A check the run must pass before it is accepted. Each gate here is a separate tool.
Rubric
The reviewed list of what a run must and must not do.
Pinned baseline
The score of an approved run, frozen so later runs can be compared with it.
Tolerance
How far a new score may fall below the baseline before it fails. Here 0.05.
Policy replay
Running the recorded tool calls through a policy engine afterwards, to see what it would have allowed.
Default deny
Anything the policy does not allow by name is refused.
Hand-over record
What one agent or person leaves for the next: what was proved, what is pending, when to stop.
Fleiss kappa
How far several judges agree beyond chance. 1 is full agreement, 0 is what chance alone gives.
Self-consistency
How often a judge gives the same score when it rates the same reply again.
Red team
Attacking a system on purpose to find its weaknesses before someone else does.
Fail-closed
When evidence is missing or a tool breaks, the answer is not passed. Never passed.
Leaked cue
A hint of the expected answer in what the judges see. It makes agreement look better than it is.
Degenerate labels
Almost every reply gets the same label, so agreement figures say little.
MISSING
A required record was not there, so the tool could not run. The audit is then INCOMPLETE.
sha256
A fingerprint of a file. Change one byte and the fingerprint changes.
